00

Start here — what you're building

A ball-bot in three layers, one radio bridge, and five rules that prevent most bad evenings.

ESP32 · drive

Drive

The inner frame: main drive motor, side-to-side (S2S) steering tilt, flywheel, IMU, battery. The brain — hosts your PS3/Nav pads over Bluetooth, runs the 100 Hz balance loops, talks to everything else.

Feather 32u4 · body

Body

Dome-tilt servos, dome-spin motor + encoder, DFPlayer audio. Wired to the drive over a serial link. Keeps the dome level and perched.

ESP32 · dome

Dome

PSI / logic / HP / eye NeoPixels, linked to the drive by radio (ESP-NOW). Rides the shell on magnets. Doubles as your wireless console once the ball is sealed.

A fourth Trinket M0 imu node lives on the drive frame and streams pitch/roll to the drive board. And ball isn't hardware — it's the dome's USB port acting as a radio bridge into a sealed drive.

The one law of bring-up

What's on the chip is what matters — not what's in the repo, not what the seller flashed. Every board answers version with PREFIX | revision | build N | date | git. A board that says nothing, or the wrong thing, is running old firmware — flash it before believing anything else.

  1. One program per COM port. Close every monitor before you flash.
  2. Boot level. The drive zeroes its angles at power-on — and again whenever you open its USB port.
  3. Believe the banner. After any flash, version must show the new build number. That read-back is the only proof a flash landed.
  4. Servos get their own 6 V. Never run the tilt servos off the 5 V logic feed — it browns out and resets the body.
  5. Sign before speed. A balance loop with a reversed motor doesn't balance — it shoves. Prove every motor's polarity before you tune.
01

Assemble the drive

Six sub-assemblies, then integrate. Full part-by-part detail — gear ratios, part numbers, backlash — lives in docs/Assembly_Drive.md; this is the sequence and the checks that matter.

  1. A — Gantry (the structural core). Close the mirrored DriveGear casings around the drive-gear train; mount the speaker pair in their pockets before the casings block access.
  2. B — Drive gear train. 26T motor gear on the ROBOTZONE planetary shaft, 47T on the output (1.81:1). Set backlash with a strip of paper between teeth while tightening. Fit the chain to the shell sprocket and tension it to a few mm of mid-span deflection — tight eats bearings, loose skips under torque.
  3. C — S2S (steering) gears. 22T on the worm-gearbox output, 69T sector on the gantry pivot (3.14:1). The worm holds the tilt without power — that's why the droid doesn't flop when disabled. Swing lock-to-lock by hand.
  4. D — Swing arms + position feedback. S2SArm and FlyWheelArm on 608ZZ bearings. Mesh the 55T gantry-pot gear to the 46T pot gear on the B10K pot. Wire the wiper to the drive ESP32 GPIO34 with a 100 nF cap wiper→GND.
  5. E — Flywheel & ballast deck. Bolt the LazySusan base underneath; add ballast and the battery harness, routing every lead through Cable Management before you close anything. Keep the mass low and centered — this deck is the pendulum that makes balance work.
  6. F — Head-tilt mast & dome spin. Press the two 1-inch bearings into the coupler, fit the mast, join the dome-spin motor via the coupler. Attach the two tilt servos (body pins 11/12, neutral 70°/110°).
Pot centering — do this now, mechanically

With the gantry mechanically level, the S2S pot must sit near the middle of its travel — never railed at 0 or 4095 anywhere in the swing. If it rails, re-clock the pot gear a tooth at a time. You'll fine-find the exact electrical center in Phase 04 with cfg autocenter, but the mechanism has to allow a centered pot first.

Integration order

A gantry → B drive gears → close casings → C S2S gears + motor → D swing arms + pot (re-check free swing with motors meshed) → E flywheel/ballast deck underneath, battery in → F head-tilt mast on top. Mount the electronics last: drive ESP32, body 32u4, and the Trinket IMU rigid, flat, and square — a tilted IMU is a permanent false lean no tuning will fix.

Assemble on a stand, not the floor

Everything from here through first tuning happens with the ball on a roller cradle or stand. The floor is for later, after the signs and gains are proven.

02

Flash the four boards

Install the PC tool once, then flash one board at a time and verify each banner.

Install the bb8 tool

Grab an installer from Releases — three roads, same bb8 at the end:

  • Setup-BASIC (most people): flashes boards from prebuilt binaries, updates over HTTPS. No git, no Arduino toolchain, no compiling.
  • Setup-MAX (you'll edit firmware): adds the arduino-cli toolchain + a GitHub link so bb8 update pulls source.
  • Clone (dev): git clone, then .\install.ps1 builds the CLI (needs .NET SDK 10+ and arduino-cli).
bb8 list # the targets + your USB serial ports, with board guesses

Flash — one board at a time

Plug in one board at a time the first time through (the two ESP32s share a USB chip, and a never-flashed board can't identify itself by banner). Suggested order — the drive is the hub, so with it running you can watch each link come alive from one console:

bb8 upload drive --port COMx # then: imu, body, dome

On a BASIC install this flashes the prebuilt release binary automatically (esptool for the ESP32s, native AVR109 for the 32u4, UF2 copy for the Trinket). Wait for the verify line each time:

Proof it landed

[VERIFY] OK — <board> is running build N. That read-back of the boot banner is the only proof — the 32u4 and Trinket don't acknowledge a flash any other way. No line, or the wrong build number, means try again.

Once a board runs this firmware, plain bb8 upload <target> auto-detects it — --port is only for first flashes and ties.

If the 32u4 / Trinket won't flash

butterfly_recv failed: close any open monitor, double-tap the board's reset (the LED pulses = bootloader, often on a different COM number), then re-run the upload immediately against that port.

03

Introduce the boards

Radio addresses and controllers — a fresh board set has fresh MACs and no paired pads.

ESP-NOW MACs (drive ↔ dome)

  1. On the drive banner (bb8 monitor drive), read [ESP-NOW] Drive WiFi MAC: XX:XX:… and write it down.
  2. On the dome (bb8 monitor dome), read its [MAC] Dome WiFi STA MAC:…. Then, on that same dome console, teach it the drive's MAC — saved to flash, survives reboots and reflashes:
    setmac XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX # the DRIVE's WiFi MAC from step 1
  3. Tell the drive the dome's MAC — either compile domeMACAddress[] into the drive sketch, or set it live and persist it:
    dome mac XX:XX:XX:XX:XX:XX # dome STA MAC; saved to NVS
Link check

With a pad connected, play any sound. The PSI pulsing white in time with it means the radio link is live. (Sounds with a dark PSI = link down.)

Pair your controller(s)

bb8 pair # guided PS3/Nav pairing + primary/secondary bb8 pair --install-driver # once, if the libusb driver isn't installed

A fresh drive board is a fresh Bluetooth host — pads paired to an old board must be re-paired. bt prefer drive|dome <MAC|slot0|slot1|none> on the drive console pins which pad is which.

04

Calibrate — before anything moves

Find the steering center, zero the level, and prove the motor signs. Skip these and the balance loop fights a ghost.

The easy path

New to it? Type setup on the drive console for a guided wizard that walks this whole phase — autocenter → level → sign check → save — one prompt at a time, and prints the exact fix if a sign is backwards. The steps below are what it automates.

Auto-find the S2S center — new, and the easy way on a fresh build

The gearbox holds the steering frame wherever you leave it, so its resting pose is not the center. cfg autocenter finds the true center for you: it drives the S2S to both mechanical stops, takes the midpoint, saves it to flash, and parks the frame there. It survives reboots, and you can re-run it any time.

cfg autocenter # drives to both stops → saves midpoint as center
Hands clear — it drives the motor

This actively runs the S2S motor to both hard stops. Keep hands and cables out of the swing path. It prints one compact line — low=… high=… center=… SAVED+parked (kept short so the one line that matters survives the best-effort dome bridge) — and fails safe if the pot barely moves (a wiring or mesh problem). On the reference build it read low 336 · high 1322 · center 829 — your numbers will differ; that's expected, the center is per-build.

Zero the level

Set the droid level and still, then:

cfg calibrate # 3 s: pitch + roll zeros (pot center kept from autocenter)

A zero-offset error looks to the PID like a permanent lean — no gain ever fixes it, so calibrate first, always. cfg calibrate drive / cfg calibrate s2s redo one axis; cfg show displays what's stored.

Sign checks — never skip on new wiring

  1. Enable (tap PS) with autoBalance on (press CROSS), and nudge the shell a few degrees by hand. Each axis should push against your nudge, back toward level.
  2. A correction that pushes with the tilt = that motor's leads are swapped. Fix it (swap leads or the sign at the H-bridge), recalibrate, retest.
  3. The relay autotuner doubles as a sign detector: if autotune drive / autotune s2s runs away instead of settling, the plant sign is inverted — confirm with a negative amplitude (autotune drive -60), then fix the wiring. Experiments auto-abort past 15°, on a joystick grab, or after 25 s.
05

First moves & the dome

On the cradle: check the dome stays perched, then the first driven moves.

The dome stays level on its own

The dome tilt servos counter your body's roll and pitch so the dome stays upright and perched on top of the ball — and this now runs whether or not autoBalance is on. On the body board, autoBalance never controlled the ball balance (that's the drive board); it only gated this dome-level, so it's decoupled now: the dome is always level while the drive is enabled.

Enable the drive, leave autoBalance off, and tilt the whole body by hand — the dome should counter-rotate to stay upright. If it tilts the wrong way, flip that axis:

tilt invert x # roll axis (left/right) tilt invert y # pitch axis (front/back) tilt save # persist to EEPROM

Slow it so it doesn't buck the dome off

A fast servo lurch can throw the magnet-riding dome off its perch. Two live knobs cap and smooth the motion — no reflash:

Dome tilt tuning — body console, tilt save to persist
commandrangewhat it does
tilt slew20–900 °/sHard cap on servo speed. Lower = gentler. Reference build runs 90 (from 220).
tilt alpha0.05–1.0Smoothing on the target. Lower = softer approach. Reference build runs 0.22.
tilt gain0.1–3.0Leveling strength (servo° per body°). 1.0 = holds level 1:1. Raise if it drifts off, lower if it over-tilts.
tilt leansigned °Leans the dome against travel so acceleration doesn't carry it over the nose. Default −8; go more negative if driving still throws it forward.

First driven moves

  • Left stick ↑↓ — drive forward/back; the dome auto-leans against the motion to stay perched.
  • Left stick ←→ — steer (S2S tilt).
  • CROSS — autoBalance (drive stabilization) on/off. Tap PS to enable/disable; hold PS 2 s to force-disable before switching the pad off.

Keep it on the cradle until steering, balance signs, and the dome all behave. Then tune: bb8 tune s2s then bb8 tune drive (full method in docs/RigTuning.md). Rig rule — keep drive Ki = 0 on rollers; add it back on the floor.

06

Save your configuration

Three stores hold your tune. Know where each lives — and take a portable backup so a reflash or board swap never costs you the evening.

Where settings live

Save as you go

Live tweaks (a pid set, a tilt slew) change RAM immediately but are lost on reboot until you commit them. Run cfg save on the drive and tilt save on the body once you're happy. cfg autocenter saves itself.

One portable backup for everything

Capture the drive's entire tuned state to a file — replay it after any reflash or onto a swapped board:

bb8 backup my-droid.txt # dump every setting to a replayable file bb8 restore my-droid.txt # pour it back after a reflash / board swap

Works over USB or through the dome's wireless bridge. Board swap becomes: flash → restore → done.

You're set up

Daily flow from here: bb8 update --flash before a session pulls new firmware for any board that's behind, then bb8 doctor if anything feels off. If it ever falls over, blackbox dump on the drive console prints the last 30 s of flight data.

SVC

Sealed? Fix it from outside

The dome is a full wireless console into a closed ball — center, tune, and even correct motor polarity without ever opening it.

Plug the dome into USB (with a controller connected, so the drive isn't mid-BT-scan) and you have the drive's entire console over the air:

bb8 monitor ball # the drive's console, over the dome's ESP-NOW bridge bb8 tune s2s --port <domeCOM> # the autotuner, same bridge

Everything the USB console does runs here — cfg autocenter, the PID autotuner (autotune s2s → autotune apply → pid save), bb8 backup/restore, OTA firmware, and the sign fixes below.

Autocenter over the bridge

cfg autocenter drives the S2S to both stops for ~15–20 s. It pumps the tunnel from inside that sweep and streams a ~1 Hz busy heartbeat with the live pot — so you can see it working — then the compact low/high/center result. (The relay autotune is non-blocking, so its progress and Ku/Tu results stream live too.)

Reverse a motor without re-wiring

Found a motor spinning the wrong way after the shell's on? Six runtime, saved-to-flash sign flips correct it — no leads to swap, no reflash. Because the motor reversals happen at the output, they flip balance and throttle together, so the loop stays stable instead of running away. Order matters: get a stable hold first, then fix direction.

Sign fixes — drive console (persist in NVS, captured by bb8 backup)
commandwhen to use it
pref revdrive on|off1 · stability. Drive runs away under balance, or throttle is backwards — flips the drive motor (balance + throttle together)
pref revs2s · revs2spot1 · stability. S2S slams to a stop / won't hold center — toggle exactly ONE, then re-run cfg autocenter
pref invdrivebal on|off2 · direction. Joystick is right but the drive balance pushes INTO the lean
pref invs2sbal on|off2 · direction. S2S holds center but balances the wrong way
pref invs2sstick on|off2 · direction. S2S steers the wrong way
Then lock it in

cfg show lists the live polarity/direction; bb8 backup my-droid.txt captures it. All six survive reboot and reflash.

Diagnose it from inside — and let it protect itself

selftest runs an on-board POST — IMU, both links, pot range, config — and prints PASS/WARN/FAIL per subsystem, so you can tell what's wrong in a closed ball without opening it (selftest full adds a gentle S2S nudge to prove the motor and pot work together). It's the inside-the-ball counterpart to bb8 doctor.

Two guards also run on their own, defaulting on: a fall / tip-over guard force-disables if it goes past 45° so the motors don't thrash on their side (tap PS to re-arm), and an S2S stall guard cuts the steering motor if it jams — pushing hard with the pot not moving — before it cooks. Both persist and ride along in bb8 backup; toggle with pref fallguard / pref stallguard if one ever false-trips.
